# Reflection on AI Use in ICS 314

## I. Introduction

I completed my undergraduate education just before large language model (LLM)–based AI tools became widespread. As a result, I did not have the opportunity to meaningfully incorporate AI into my learning at that time. Now, as I pursue my master’s degree, I view AI as a valuable tool that can both accelerate efficiency and significantly improve comprehension of complex subjects—when used properly.

At the same time, I have observed many people fall into the trap of allowing AI to replace their thinking entirely. In these cases, individuals rely on AI for nearly every task, make little effort to understand the underlying material, and become unable to work independently. This lack of understanding is often clearly reflected in the quality of their work. My approach to AI has been intentionally different: I treat it as a supporting tool rather than a substitute for reasoning or learning.

---

## II. Personal Experience with AI in ICS 314

*Note: WOD stands for “Workout of the Day,” which are time-constrained technical or coding interview–style problems.*

### Experience WODs (e.g., E18)

I used AI to write and integrate code for Experience WODs. Having AI generate most of the code allowed me to focus more on higher-level reasoning and overall problem structure rather than implementation details. Troubleshooting initial AI-generated solutions often took a significant amount of time, but I expect this overhead to decrease as my prompt engineering improves.

### In-class Practice WODs

I used AI to assist with writing and integrating code during in-class Practice WODs. This helped me iterate more quickly and focus on recognizing patterns across problems. However, AI-generated code still required debugging and verification, which sometimes reduced the overall time savings.

### In-class WODs

I used AI for in-class WODs to help generate code solutions. This was useful for accelerating development, but the results always required careful review and correction to ensure correctness and alignment with course expectations.

### Essays

I used AI to rewrite my own writing into clearer and more coherent prose while preserving my original voice and ideas. I also used AI to help generate potential topics when I felt stuck during the writing process. This improved efficiency without replacing my thinking.

### Final Project

For the final project, most of the code implementations were generated by AI. This significantly reduced the time spent on low-level implementation work and allowed me to focus on higher-level system architecture and design decisions. All AI-generated code was reviewed and adapted to meet project requirements.

### Learning a Concept / Tutorial

I used AI to explain complicated topics in ways that helped me learn them more efficiently. This was especially useful when encountering unfamiliar paradigms or abstractions.

### Answering a Question in Class or in Discord

I occasionally used AI to answer questions in class, specifically when the professor explicitly prompted us to ask AI to generate responses. Outside of those situations, I did not rely on AI for discussion.

### Asking or Answering a Smart Question

I did not use AI to ask or answer smart questions. I believe it is important to have a solid understanding of a problem before attempting to troubleshoot it, especially when the issue is critical to something like the final project.

### Coding Example

I used AI to generate coding examples when learning new concepts, such as prototypical object-oriented programming. These examples helped make abstract ideas more concrete, though they sometimes required adjustment to match course conventions.

### Explaining Code

When AI-generated solutions were unclear or overly opaque, I asked AI to explain the code step by step. This ensured I fully understood all aspects of the work I submitted.

### Writing Code

I used AI to write code throughout the course, particularly for WODs and the final project. This allowed me to focus more on design and structure rather than boilerplate or repetitive implementation details.

### Documenting Code

AI-generated code was typically self-documented with comments. I reviewed these comments to ensure they accurately reflected the code’s behavior.

### Quality Assurance

I frequently used AI to fix syntax errors, typing mistakes, and file-level issues. This significantly reduced time spent on tedious debugging, though all fixes still required validation.

### Other Uses in ICS 314

I did not use AI for any additional purposes beyond those described above.

---

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding

Overall, AI had a positive impact on my learning and understanding. It helped me grasp complicated topics more effectively and allowed me to focus on larger-scale problems rather than getting stuck on low-level details. When used intentionally, AI enhanced comprehension without replacing critical thinking.

---

## IV. Practical Applications of AI Outside ICS 314

AI is particularly effective at automating repetitive tasks and processing large amounts of data. One notable example is in medicine, where researchers use AI to study protein folding. Traditionally, this required extensive computational resources, but AI-based approaches have significantly accelerated the process without the need for large-scale infrastructure.

---

## V. Challenges and Opportunities

AI is not always reliable and can hallucinate information, introduce errors, or violate non-negotiable constraints in a codebase. Understanding where and why AI fails is essential to using it effectively. Prompt engineering can help mitigate these issues, but careful verification remains necessary.

---

## VI. Comparative Analysis

Compared to traditional teaching methods, AI-enhanced approaches offer faster feedback and broader exposure to examples. However, traditional methods remain essential for deep understanding, long-term retention, and skill development. The most effective approach is a hybrid of both.

---

## VII. Future Considerations

As AI becomes a permanent part of the software engineering industry, education will need to evolve accordingly. While foundational concepts will remain essential, skills such as prompt engineering, debugging AI-generated code, and critically evaluating automated solutions will become increasingly important.

---

## VIII. Conclusion

I appreciate that this course addressed the changing landscape of software engineering through the lens of AI. However, more can be done to better prepare students for this evolving environment. Teaching AI usage should not overshadow traditional software engineering fundamentals.

At the same time, AI provides a meaningful advantage to students who use it thoughtfully and responsibly. Care should be taken to manage disparities between students who adopt AI and those who do not, while still reflecting modern industry realities.
